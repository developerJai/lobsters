# Hats

> A comprehensive guide to Hats.

---

## What is Hats?

A hat is a verified affiliation badge — like "OpenBSD Developer" — that you can wear on a comment or private message to show you are speaking officially for a project, organization, or company. Every hat is checked and granted by a moderator before it appears, so readers can trust the affiliation behind it.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Browse every active hat and its wearer on the public Hats page — no account needed
- Request a hat with a short label, a public verification link, and a private comment for moderators
- Hold multiple hats, wearing at most one on any single comment or private message
- Receive the decision as a private message with the moderator's reason, whether approved or rejected
- Give up ("doff") a hat with a short public reason when you no longer speak for the organization

---

## How It Works

1. **You request a hat** — On the Hats page (while signed in), choose **Request Hat** and fill in three things: the badge text (e.g., "XYZ Project Member"), a public link proving your affiliation (an email address at the organization, or a URL to an employment page), and a comment that is meant for moderators reviewing your request.
2. **A moderator reviews the request** — Pending requests sit in a review queue. The moderator may tidy up your hat's wording or link before approving, and must give a reason for whichever decision they make.
3. **You get the outcome by private message** — Approved: "Your hat "…" has been approved," the badge is added to your account, and the grant is recorded in the public moderation log. Rejected: "Your request for hat "…" has been rejected," with the reason. Either way, the request itself is removed from the queue — the message is your record.
4. **You wear it when speaking officially** — Hovering over a hat shows when it was granted. If the hat's link is a website, the badge itself links there (opening in a new tab).
5. **The hat is retired when the affiliation ends** — You (or a moderator) doff the hat with a short public reason like "Left project". It leaves the public Hats page and can't be worn again, but your old posts keep showing it.

> A hat's lifecycle: **Requested** → **Approved (active)** → **Doffed (retired)** — or **Requested** → **Rejected**.

## Common Questions

**Q: Who should request a hat?**
A: People who will genuinely speak officially on the site for a project, organization, or company. Hats are not for commemorating past contributions or former jobs, and they are usually reserved for notable projects and organizations.

**Q: Can I have more than one hat?**
A: Yes — you can hold several, but you can wear at most one on any given comment or private message.

**Q: What goes in the Link field?**
A: Public proof of your affiliation: your email address at the organization, or a URL to a page showing your employment. It's shown with your hat. If it's an email address, visitors who aren't signed in only ever see the domain part, never your full address.

**Q: Who can read my request comment?**
A: It's intended for moderators during review — but be aware that any signed-in user can view the pending request queue, so don't put anything truly secret in it.

**Q: What are the length limits?**
A: The hat text and link can each be up to 255 characters, and all three fields (hat, link, comment) are required.

**Q: What happens the moment my request is approved?**
A: The hat is added to your account immediately, the grant appears in the public moderation log, and you receive a private message containing the moderator's reason. The moderator may have lightly edited your hat's wording or link as part of approving it.

**Q: How do I give up my hat?**
A: You can doff your own hat (or a moderator can). A short public reason is required — e.g., "Left project" — and submitting without one fails with "You must give a reason for the doffing." After doffing you're taken back to the profile page.

**Q: Can a hat be renamed later?**
A: Yes, by moderators, in two ways: a direct correction (publicly logged as a rename or link change), or retiring the old hat and granting a fresh one with the new text — which keeps your history intact, since the old hat remains visible as retired with the note that it was replaced.

**Q: Why do my old comments still show a doffed hat?**
A: By design. History is preserved: posts keep the badge they were made with. Doffing only prevents the hat from being worn on anything new.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Choose a clear, specific badge text — "XYZ Project Member" beats something vague.
- Give moderators a link they can verify quickly; an employment page or organization email works well.
- Keep the public parts (hat text and link) clean, and put the persuasive detail in the comment — but remember the queue is visible to signed-in users.
- Doff promptly when you leave an organization; the short public reason is all that's needed.
- Don't re-request a retired hat's exact circumstances — a doffed hat can't be re-worn, so a new affiliation means a new request.

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- All three request fields are required; the hat text and link each max out at 255 characters.
- Doffing is permanent — a retired hat can never be worn again, and it disappears from the public Hats page (old posts still show it).
- Decided requests are deleted: approvals are recorded in the public moderation log, but a rejection's only record is the private message you receive.
- The public moderation log shows hat grants, edits, and doffings — including the doffing reason you provide.
- If you doff your own hat, the public log entry is not attributed to any moderator.
- The public Hats page only lists hats of active accounts; if an account goes inactive its hats drop off the list.

## Related Features

- **Comments** — the main place a hat is worn to speak officially.
- **Private Messages** — approval and rejection outcomes arrive here; hats can be worn on messages too.
- **Settings** — the request form recommends connecting your GitHub account (external section) so moderators can verify you instantly.
- **Moderation Log** — the public record of every hat grant, edit, and doffing.
- **User Profiles** — doffed hats remain visible on your profile even after leaving the public list.
