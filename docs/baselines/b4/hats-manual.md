# Hats — User Manual

> Step-by-step instructions for using Hats.

---

## Prerequisites

- Anyone, even without an account, can browse the public Hats page
- You must be signed in to request a hat or to doff one of your own hats
- Approving, rejecting, and renaming hats are moderator tasks
- Recommended before requesting: connect your GitHub account in **Settings** (external section) so moderators can verify you instantly

---

## Overview

This guide walks you through the full life of a hat — the verified affiliation badge worn on comments and private messages to show you are speaking officially for a project, organization, or company. It covers requesting a hat, understanding the decision, wearing it, and retiring ("doffing") it when your affiliation ends. Moderator variations are noted where relevant.

---

## Task 1: Request a Hat

### Steps

1. Sign in and open the **Hats** page
2. Choose **Request Hat**
3. Fill in all three required fields:
   - **Hat** — the badge text, e.g. "XYZ Project Member" (up to 255 characters). Keep it clear and specific
   - **Link** — public proof of your affiliation: your email address at the organization, or a URL to an employment page (up to 255 characters). This is shown with your hat once granted
   - **Comment** — context for the moderators reviewing your request. Note: any signed-in user can view the pending queue, so don't include anything truly secret
4. Submit the request

### Expected Result

Your request enters the moderator review queue. You don't need to do anything further — the decision will arrive as a private message.

---

## Task 2: Receive the Decision

### Steps

1. Wait for a moderator to review your request (they may lightly edit your hat's wording or link before approving)
2. Check your private messages for the outcome:
   - Approved: a message titled **"Your hat "…" has been approved"** containing the moderator's reason
   - Rejected: a message titled **"Your request for hat "…" has been rejected"** containing the reason
3. If approved, the hat is already on your account — the grant is also recorded in the public moderation log

### Expected Result

Approved hats appear on the public **Hats** page immediately. Rejected requests are removed; the private message is your only record of the decision, so keep it if you want the reasoning.

---

## Task 3: Wear Your Hat

### Steps

1. When writing a comment or private message where you're speaking for the organization, select the hat to wear
2. Remember: you may hold several hats, but you can wear at most one on any single comment or message

### Expected Result

Readers see the badge on your post. Hovering over it shows when the hat was granted; if your hat's link is a website, the badge links there and opens in a new tab. If your link is an email address, signed-out visitors only ever see its domain, never the full address.

---

## Task 4: Doff (Retire) a Hat

Do this when you no longer speak for the organization — for example, you've left the project or changed jobs. You can doff your own hat; a moderator can also doff it for you.

### Steps

1. Open the doffing page for your hat
2. Enter a short public **Reason**, such as "Left project" — this is required
3. Submit

### Expected Result

You're taken back to the profile page. The hat leaves the public Hats page and can never be worn again; the doffing and your reason appear in the public moderation log. Your old posts keep showing the hat — history is preserved. If you doff your own hat, the log entry is not attributed to any moderator.

---

## Variations

### Moderator: renaming a hat

Moderators can change a hat in two ways:

1. **Direct correction** — edit the wording or link in place; the change is publicly logged as a rename or link change
2. **Retire and replace** — doff the old hat and grant a fresh one with the new text; the old hat stays visible as retired with a note that it was replaced, keeping the wearer's history intact

### Browsing hats without an account

Anyone can open the public **Hats** page to see every active hat and its wearer. Hats belonging to inactive accounts drop off the list, as do doffed hats (old posts and profiles still show them).

---

## Settings & Configuration

Navigate to **Settings > External**

| Setting | Description | Default |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| GitHub account connection | Connecting GitHub lets moderators verify your hat request instantly | Not connected |

There are no other settings for this feature.

## Troubleshooting

### "You must give a reason for the doffing."

**Cause:** The **Reason** field was left empty when retiring a hat — a short public explanation is required.
**Solution:** Enter a brief reason such as "Left project" and submit again.

### My request won't submit

**Cause:** All three fields — **Hat**, **Link**, and **Comment** — are required, and the hat text and link are each limited to 255 characters.
**Solution:** Complete every field within the limits and resubmit.

### My hat disappeared from the Hats page

**Cause:** Either the hat was doffed (by you or a moderator), or the account is no longer active — the public list only shows active hats of active accounts.
**Solution:** Check your private messages and the public moderation log for a doffing entry and its reason. Old posts and your profile still show the hat.

### I want my old hat back

**Cause:** Doffing is permanent — a retired hat can never be worn again.
**Solution:** If you've genuinely regained the affiliation, submit a new hat request (Task 1).

### I was rejected and lost the explanation

**Cause:** Decided requests are deleted; a rejection's only record is the private message.
**Solution:** The reason exists only in that message. If you've deleted it, you may submit a new request with stronger verification — e.g., a GitHub-verified organization link.
