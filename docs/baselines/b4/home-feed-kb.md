# Home Feed

> A comprehensive guide to the Home Feed.

---

## What is Home Feed?

The home feed is the set of story lists you browse on the site. The front page shows the "hottest" stories, and the same stories can be viewed in other orders — newest, most recently discussed, or top-rated over a time period — or narrowed to a topic tag, a group of tags, a category, a particular website or source, or one member's submissions. You can hide topics you don't want to see anywhere on the site, and follow almost any list from a feed reader.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Browse stories as Hottest (front page), Newest, Recent (new stories that haven't reached the front page yet), Active (sorted by the latest comment), or Top (best of a time period, one week by default)
- Narrow a list to one tag, several tags at once, a category, a website, a source, or a single member's newest submissions
- Hide whole topics everywhere with tag filters — this works even without an account
- Keep personal lists of stories you've saved, upvoted, or hidden (sign-in required)
- Subscribe by RSS, including private feeds that apply your tag filters
- Pick up where you left off on Newest with a "Last Read" marker that even carries onto later pages

---

## How It Works

1. **Choose a view** — The front page ranks stories by hotness. Newest shows everything in submission order. Recent shows the newest stories that have not yet reached the front page. Active sorts by the most recent comment. Top shows the best stories of a period — the default is one week, and you can change the period in the address (for example five days or two years); an unrecognized period just takes you back to the one-week view.
2. **Narrow it down** — Open a tag page to see one topic; its header describes the tag, mentions if it raises or lowers story ranking, notes special rules (some tags are moderator-only, some can't be used by brand-new members), and suggests related tags. You can combine several tags, or several categories, by separating names with commas. Website and source pages show everything submitted from one place, along with story and submitter counts and whether the source has been banned.
3. **Your filters apply everywhere** — Any tag you've hidden is removed from every list you view, automatically.
4. **Read and act** — Each row shows the story's score, title, tags, source site, submitter, age, and comment count. Signed-in members can hide, save, flag, or suggest changes right from the list, and move through pages with the page links at the bottom.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why is a story missing from my feeds when others can see it?**
A: One of its tags is probably in your filters, or you hid the story. Check the Filtered Tags page and your Hidden list.

**Q: Can I filter topics without an account?**
A: Yes. Save your choices on the Filtered Tags page and they're kept in a long-lasting browser cookie. They apply only in that browser — sign in to keep filters on your account.

**Q: What's the difference between Newest and Recent?**
A: Newest is every story in submission order. Recent is only the newest stories that haven't reached the front page yet.

**Q: How do I change the Top page's time period?**
A: Edit the period in the address — for example five days (`/top/5d`) or two years (`/top/2y`). Unknown periods redirect to the default week.

**Q: What does the "Last Read" line on Newest mean?**
A: When you're signed in, it marks the first story you had already seen on your previous visit, so everything above it is new to you. If it isn't on the first page, it carries over to later pages.

**Q: How do private RSS feeds work?**
A: The front page, Newest, Saved, and Upvoted feeds accept a personal token in the link. With the token, the feed applies your tag filters and is titled "Private feed for *your-username*". Treat the link like a password.

**Q: Why does the Recent page's feed point at Newest?**
A: On purpose — the Recent list constantly changes as stories get upvoted onto the front page, so the stable Newest feed is advertised instead.

**Q: Why did a comma-separated tag or category address return "not found"?**
A: Every name in the list must exist. A single misspelled name makes the whole page fail.

**Q: Why do pages look slightly out of date when I'm signed out?**
A: Signed-out visitors get briefly cached pages — story lists up to about 45 seconds old and the front-page feed up to about 2 minutes. Signed-in members always see live lists.

## Tips & Best Practices

- The Filtered Tags page shows each tag's description, how many stories carry it, and how many people filter it — useful signals when deciding what to hide.
- Use Recent to discover and upvote stories before they hit the front page.
- On tag pages, check the related-tags suggestions to find adjacent topics worth following.
- Subscribe with your private token links so your feed reader sees your filtered view; confirm the feed title says "Private feed for" you.
- Use Saved as a reading queue — it has its own private feed.

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- Saved, Upvoted, and Hidden lists require signing in.
- Cookie-based filters don't follow you across browsers or devices.
- Some tags can't be filtered — those rows simply have no Hide checkbox, and such choices are ignored if submitted anyway.
- The Top view doesn't offer a JSON version, and Recent and Hidden are web-page only.
- Very large or negative page numbers are rejected.
- Signed-out browsing is briefly cached (see above); this is by design for speed.
