# Hats: Showing Who You Speak For

A **hat** is a small, verified badge on your Lobsters account that shows you are speaking officially for a project, organization, or company — for example, "OpenBSD Developer" or "XYZ Project Member". When you wear a hat on a comment or private message, other readers know your words carry that affiliation.

## What hats are (and aren't)

Hats exist for making public statements on behalf of a project or company. They are **not** a trophy case: a hat is not meant to commemorate past contributions or jobs you once held. You should only request one if you will actually be acting in that official capacity on the site — for example, you're a core developer of a project or an employee authorized to speak for your organization. Hats are usually reserved for notable projects and organizations.

You can hold more than one hat, but you can only wear one at a time on any given comment or message.

## Where hats appear

- Every active hat is listed on the public **Hats** page, grouped by hat name, showing the wearer and a verification link.
- On comments and messages, a worn hat appears as a small labelled badge. Hovering over it shows when the hat was granted. If the hat is backed by a website link, clicking the badge opens that site; if it's backed by an email address, visitors who aren't logged in only see the email's domain, never the full address.

## Requesting a hat

1. Sign in and open the Hats page, then choose **Request Hat**.
2. Fill in three things:
   - **Hat** — the short label you want, e.g. "XYZ Project Member".
   - **Link** — public proof of your affiliation: your email address at that project or company, or a URL to a page showing your employment. This link is shown to users who hover over your hat.
   - **Comment** — private notes seen only by moderators while they review your request. Linking to discussions where the hat would be useful helps your case.
3. Submit the request. You'll see a confirmation, and a moderator will review it.

**Tip:** connecting your GitHub account in your settings and linking (in your comment) to where the organization lists that account lets moderators verify you instantly, without a verification email.

## What happens after you ask

A moderator reviews your request and either approves or rejects it. Either way, you receive a private message telling you the outcome and the moderator's reasoning:

- **Approved** — the hat is added to your account immediately and appears on the public Hats page. The approval is also recorded in the public moderation log.
- **Rejected** — you get a message explaining why. You can submit a new request later if circumstances change.

Moderators may lightly adjust the hat's wording or link before approving it.

## Giving up a hat ("doffing")

When you can no longer speak for the project or company — you've left the team, changed jobs, and so on — the hat should be **doffed** (retired). You can doff your own hat, or contact a moderator to do it; a short public reason is required, like "Left project". 

Once doffed:

- The hat disappears from the public Hats table (your profile page still records it).
- You can no longer wear it on new comments or messages.
- Your **old** comments and messages keep showing the hat — history is preserved, not rewritten.
- The doffing, with its reason, is recorded in the public moderation log.

## Common questions

**Can I have several hats?** Yes — but only one can be worn on any single comment or private message.

**Is wearing a hat mandatory?** No. Having a hat just gives you the option of speaking officially when it's relevant.

**Who sees my request comment?** Only moderators, during approval. The hat label and the link, however, are public once granted.

**Can a hat's name change?** Moderators can correct a hat's wording or link. For bigger changes they retire the old hat and grant a fresh one, so past posts keep the label they were made under.

**Why does my old hat still show on old posts?** By design: the site preserves what affiliation you claimed at the time you posted.
