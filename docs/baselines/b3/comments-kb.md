# Comments

> A comprehensive guide to Comments.

---

## What is Comments?

Comments are how you join the conversation under any story on the site. You can reply to other people, and replies nest into threads so discussions stay organized. The community's votes decide which comments appear first, and you're notified whenever someone replies to you or mentions your username.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Write comments with Markdown formatting, and preview them before posting
- Reply to any comment — threads nest up to 18 levels deep
- Upvote comments you find valuable, or take your vote back
- Flag rule-breaking comments for the moderators, with a reason
- Edit your comment for up to 6 hours, delete it for up to 14 days, or disown it later
- Wear one of your hats (affiliation badges) on a comment
- Get reply and mention notifications in your inbox, by email, or as phone push notifications
- Browse the newest comments site-wide, the comments you've upvoted, and your own recent threads — with RSS feeds

---

## How It Works

1. **Write** — Open a story, type into the comment box, and use **Preview** to see the formatted result. Press **Post** when you're happy.
2. **Quality check** — The site automatically declines comments that don't add anything: "me too", "+1", "this", "tl;dr", thread bumps, quote-only or emoji-only comments, and empty comments. You'll get a short message explaining why.
3. **It goes live** — Your comment appears immediately with one point (your own automatic vote). On a brand-new comment the score stays hidden for the first 36 hours while it's near zero, to keep voting honest.
4. **People respond** — Others can upvote, flag, or reply. The best-received replies in each group float to the top automatically.
5. **You're notified** — Replies to your comments (and to your stories, if you follow them) and @mentions of your username land in your inbox, plus email or push if you've turned those on. You can even answer the notification email to post your reply.

A comment's lifecycle in plain terms:
> **Posted** → (optionally **Edited**, within 6 hours) → stays up, or → **Deleted by you** (within 14 days, and you can undelete) / **Removed by a moderator** (with a public reason) / **Disowned by you** (after 14 days — it stays but is no longer yours)

## Common Questions

**Q: How long can I edit my comment?**
A: Six hours from posting (or from your last edit). If you edit more than a minute after posting, the comment gets an "edited" label so readers know.

**Q: Can I delete a comment?**
A: Yes, within 14 days. It will show "Comment removed by author" in the thread. You can undelete it yourself, unless a moderator removed it. After 14 days you can no longer delete, but you can disown it so it's no longer attributed to you.

**Q: Why was my comment rejected with a message like "Please just upvote the parent post instead."?**
A: The site declines comments that only say "me too", "nice", or "+1" — upvoting the parent expresses the same thing without adding noise. Similar rules decline "this", "tl;dr", bumps, quote-only, and emoji-only comments.

**Q: Why can't I see a comment's score?**
A: Scores on comments less than 36 hours old are hidden while they're near zero (between −2 and +4), to prevent pile-on voting. Scores also stay hidden on comments you've flagged.

**Q: Why am I being told to wait before replying?**
A: In threads that are collecting flags, everyone has to pace their comments — the wait starts around two minutes and grows with the number of flags (and grows faster if you flagged there yourself). It's the site's way of cooling heated arguments.

**Q: Why can't I flag this comment?**
A: You can't flag your own comments, comments older than 7 days, or comments already voted down to −10. Flagging always requires picking a reason from the list. And once you flag a comment, you can't reply to it — you've handed it to the moderators.

**Q: What's the deepest a thread can go?**
A: 18 levels. Past that, the reply link disappears and you'll see a note suggesting the discussion has run its course — perhaps blog about it and submit that instead.

**Q: Who gets notified when I comment?**
A: The author of the comment you replied to, the story's author (if they follow their story), and anyone you @mention. Nobody is notified twice for the same comment — a reply notification takes priority over a mention.

**Q: What happens when a moderator removes my comment?**
A: It shows "Comment removed by moderator" with the moderator's name and reason, the removal appears in the public moderation log, and you lose karma in proportion to the votes the comment had.

**Q: Is there a way to follow the newest comments site-wide?**
A: Yes — the Newest Comments page lists them 20 per page with a "Last Read" marker showing where you left off, and offers RSS feeds including a private one matched to your tag filters.

## Tips & Best Practices

- **Preview before posting** — Markdown mistakes are easier to catch before they're live, and editing later adds an "edited" label.
- **Upvote instead of posting "+1"** — agreement comments are declined anyway; your vote carries the signal.
- **Don't reply to something you flagged** — the site won't let you, by design. Let the moderators handle it.
- **Wear a hat when speaking in an official capacity** — pick it from the "Put on hat:" selector so readers know which role you're speaking in.
- **Hide a story to silence its comment emails** — you'll stop getting email and push notifications about it, but inbox notifications quietly accumulate in case you return.
- **Use the permanent comment link** (the timestamp) when sharing a comment — it keeps working even if the story is merged into another.

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- Comment threads stop at 18 levels — no exceptions.
- The 6-hour edit and 14-day delete windows can't be extended.
- Heavily downvoted comments (−5 and below) appear collapsed; readers must click to expand them.
- The upvoted-comments page currently doesn't filter out stories you've hidden.
- If an identical comment is submitted twice (slow connection, double-click), the site quietly keeps just one — this is intentional.
- Very old stories stop accepting comments; the comment box warns you when a story is minutes from closing.
- Notification emails can occasionally fail to send; the inbox notification is always there as the reliable record.
