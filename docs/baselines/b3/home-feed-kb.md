# Home Feed

> A comprehensive guide to the Home Feed.

---

## What is Home Feed?

The home feed is how you browse stories on the site. The front page shows the "hottest" stories right now, and the same list comes in other flavors — newest, most recently discussed, or top-rated over a time period — as well as narrowed views for a single topic tag, a group of tags, a category, a particular website, or one member's submissions. You can hide topics you don't want to see, and follow any list from your feed reader.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Browse stories five ways: Hottest (front page), Newest, Recent, Active (latest discussion), and Top (best of a time period)
- Narrow any view to a tag, several tags at once, a category, a specific website or source, or a single member's submissions
- Hide entire topics everywhere on the site with tag filters — this works even before you create an account
- Keep personal lists of stories you've saved, upvoted, or hidden
- Subscribe to almost any list by RSS, including private feeds that respect your tag filters
- Pick up where you left off on the Newest page with a "Last Read" marker

---

## How It Works

1. **Pick a view** — The front page ranks stories by a "hotness" blend of votes, discussion, and age. Newest lists everything in submission order, Recent highlights new stories that haven't hit the front page yet, Active sorts by the latest comment, and Top shows the highest-rated stories of the past week (you can change the period, e.g. five days or two years, by editing the address).
2. **Narrow it if you like** — Open a tag to see only stories on that topic (the page also describes the tag, notes any special rules, and suggests related tags). You can list several tags, or categories, separated by commas. Website and source pages collect everything submitted from one place, with story and submitter counts.
3. **Your filters shape everything** — Any tag you've filtered is removed from every list you view, automatically.
4. **Read and act** — Each story row shows its score, title, tags, source site, who submitted it and when, plus its comment count. Signed-in members can hide, save, flag, or suggest changes right from the list, and move between pages with the Page links at the bottom.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why don't I see a story that other people are discussing?**
A: Most likely one of its tags is in your filters, or you hid the story yourself. Check the Filtered Tags page and your Hidden list.

**Q: Do I need an account to filter out topics?**
A: No. Pick your tags on the Filtered Tags page and save — your choices are kept in a long-lasting browser cookie. They only apply to that browser, though; sign in to store them permanently on your account across devices.

**Q: What's the difference between Newest and Recent?**
A: Newest is everything in submission order. Recent shows only the newest stories that haven't yet reached the front page — a place to find things that still need votes.

**Q: How do I change the time period on the Top page?**
A: Edit the address — for example `/top/5d` for five days or `/top/2y` for two years. The page lists the units it understands. An unrecognized period simply takes you to the default, the past week.

**Q: What is the "Last Read" line on Newest?**
A: When you're signed in, the site remembers your last visit to Newest and draws a **Last Read** divider above the first story you'd already seen — and it carries over to later pages if needed.

**Q: Can I follow these lists in my feed reader?**
A: Yes — the front page, Newest, Top, tag pages, category pages, and website pages all have RSS feeds. Signed-in members also get private feed links containing a personal token; those apply your tag filters and are titled "Private feed for *your-username*". Treat that link like a password.

**Q: Why does a story show `~` instead of a score?**
A: Scores are deliberately hidden on some stories for some viewers to keep voting fair.

**Q: Why did a page of several tags say "not found"?**
A: Every name in a comma-separated tag or category address must exist. One misspelled name makes the whole page return "not found".

## Tips & Best Practices

- Filter aggressively: the Filtered Tags page shows each tag's description, how many stories carry it, and how many members filter it — good signals for what's worth hiding.
- Use Recent to find and upvote promising stories before they hit the front page.
- On a tag page, check the "most often also tagged with" suggestions to discover adjacent topics, and note hints like a tag that raises or lowers story ranking.
- Subscribe to your private RSS links (linked from the Filtered Tags page, including an all-comments feed) so your reader sees exactly what you'd see on the site.
- Use the Saved list as a reading queue — it has its own private feed too.

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- Signed-out browsing is briefly cached: lists can be up to about 45 seconds old and the front-page feed up to 2 minutes old. Sign in for always-live, personalized lists.
- Cookie-based filters don't follow you to another browser or device.
- The Recent page's feed link intentionally points at the Newest feed, because Recent constantly changes as stories get promoted.
- The Hidden, Saved, and Upvoted lists require signing in.
- Some special tags can't be filtered (they have no Hide checkbox), and some tags can only be used by moderators or are off-limits to brand-new members — tag pages say so.
- Very large or negative page numbers in the address are rejected.
