# Hats

> A comprehensive guide to Hats.

---

## What is Hats?

A hat is a verified badge — like "OpenBSD Developer" — that you can wear on a comment or private message to show you are speaking officially for a project, organization, or company. Moderators check every hat before granting it, so readers can trust that a hat-wearing comment really comes from someone authorized to speak for that organization.

---

## Key Capabilities

- Browse all active hats and their wearers on the public Hats page
- Request a hat for your account with a label, a public verification link, and a private note to moderators
- Hold multiple hats, wearing one at a time on any comment or private message
- Receive a private message with the moderator's reasoning when your request is approved or rejected
- Give up ("doff") a hat with a short public reason when you no longer speak for the organization

---

## How It Works

1. **You request a hat** — From the Hats page, choose Request Hat and provide three things: the hat's label (e.g., "XYZ Project Member"), a public link proving your affiliation (your email address at the project, or a page showing your employment), and a private comment that only moderators will see while reviewing.
2. **A moderator reviews it** — Moderators verify you're genuinely authorized to speak for the organization. They may tidy up the label or link before approving.
3. **You get the decision by private message** — Approved: the hat appears on your account and in the public hats list, and the grant is recorded in the public moderation log. Rejected: the message explains why.
4. **You wear it when it matters** — When commenting or messaging, you can choose to wear one of your hats. Readers hovering over the badge see when it was granted, and can follow its link.
5. **You retire it when the affiliation ends** — The hat is "doffed" with a short public reason (e.g., "Left project"). Old posts keep showing it, but it can't be worn again.

> Hats move through a simple lifecycle: **Requested** → **Approved (active)** → **Doffed (retired)** — or **Requested** → **Rejected**.

## Common Questions

**Q: Who should request a hat?**
A: Only people who will actually speak officially on the site — e.g., a core project developer or an employee authorized to post on behalf of their company. Hats are usually reserved for notable projects and organizations; they are not for commemorating past contributions or former jobs.

**Q: Can I have more than one hat?**
A: Yes, you can hold several, but you can only wear one at a time on any given comment or private message.

**Q: What should I put in the Link field?**
A: Public proof of your affiliation: your email address at the project or company, or a URL to a page showing your employment. This link is shown publicly with your hat. If you use an email address, visitors who aren't signed in only see its domain, never the full address.

**Q: Who sees my request comment?**
A: Only moderators, during review. Use it to link to discussions where the hat would be useful and to evidence of your role.

**Q: How can I speed up approval?**
A: Connect your GitHub account in your settings, and in your request comment link to where the organization publicly lists that account. Moderators can then verify you instantly without sending a verification email.

**Q: What happens when my request is decided?**
A: You receive a private message either way — "Your hat … has been approved" or "Your request for hat … has been rejected" — including the moderator's reason. Decided requests don't stay in a queue; the message is your record.

**Q: What happens when I leave the project or company?**
A: Contact a moderator to doff your hat (or doff it yourself) with a short public reason. Your old comments still show the hat, but you won't be able to use it again — if you rejoin later, you'd request a new one.

**Q: Can a hat be renamed?**
A: Moderators can correct a hat's text or link. For a real change of affiliation they retire the old hat and grant a new one, so historical posts keep the label they were posted under.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Pick a clear, specific label — "XYZ Project Member" tells readers more than something vague.
- Use a verification link moderators can check in seconds; a GitHub-verifiable organization listing is the fastest path.
- Put the persuasive detail in the private comment (it's moderator-only), and keep the public label and link clean.
- Wear your hat only when you're actually speaking for the organization — that's what keeps the signal meaningful.
- Doff promptly when you leave: a short reason like "Left project" is all that's needed, and it's shown publicly.

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- A hat request requires all three fields — label, link, and comment — and labels/links have modest length limits.
- Doffing is permanent: a doffed hat cannot be re-worn, and it disappears from the public hats table (your profile still records it).
- Old comments and messages keep displaying the hat they were posted with — history is preserved, not rewritten.
- Grants and doffings appear in the public moderation log, including the doffing reason you give.
- Rejected requests are removed once decided; keep the moderator's message if you want the reasoning.

## Related Features

- **Comments** — the main place you wear a hat to speak officially.
- **Private Messages** — approval and rejection notices arrive here; hats can be worn on messages too.
- **Settings** — connecting your GitHub account here speeds up hat verification.
- **Moderation Log** — public record of every hat grant, edit, and doffing.
