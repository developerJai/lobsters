# Home Feed

> A comprehensive guide to browsing and discovering content on Lobsters.

---

## What is Home Feed?

The home feed is the main way you browse content on Lobsters. It provides several different views of submitted stories: the front page (ranked by popularity and engagement), newest submissions, active discussions, stories you might have missed, top stories over a time period, and more. Your tag filters are applied to all feeds so you only see topics you care about.

---

## Key Capabilities

- **Hottest (Front Page)** -- The default homepage showing the best current stories ranked by engagement
- **Newest** -- All stories in chronological order, newest first
- **Active** -- Stories with the most recent comment activity
- **Recent** -- Stories from the past 10 days that have not reached the front page
- **Top** -- Highest-scoring stories over a customizable time period (day, week, month, year)
- **Saved** -- Your bookmarked stories
- **Hidden** -- Stories you chose to hide
- **Upvoted** -- Stories you have upvoted
- **By Tag** -- Stories filtered to one or more specific tags
- **By Category** -- Stories filtered by a tag category
- **By Domain** -- Stories from a specific website
- **By User** -- A specific user's submissions
- RSS feeds available for most views
- "Last read" marker on the Newest page so you can pick up where you left off

---

## How It Works

1. Visit the Lobsters homepage to see the "Hottest" feed -- stories ranked by a combination of votes, comments, and age.
2. Use the navigation links to switch between Newest, Active, Recent, or other views.
3. Your tag filters are automatically applied to all feeds, hiding topics you have filtered out.
4. Stories are shown 25 per page. Click "More" at the bottom to load the next page.
5. If you are logged in, each story shows your voting state (whether you upvoted or flagged it) and whether you have hidden or saved it.
6. The Newest page remembers where you left off with a "last read" marker, so you can easily catch up on new submissions.
7. Use the Top page with time intervals like `/top/1d` (one day), `/top/1w` (one week), or `/top/1m` (one month) to find the best stories over a specific period.
8. Subscribe to RSS feeds for any view to follow updates in your feed reader.

## Common Questions

**Q: How does the front page ranking work?**
A: Stories are ranked by a "hotness" algorithm that factors in upvotes, comment engagement, time since posting, and tag modifiers. Newer stories with high engagement rise to the top, while older stories gradually fall off.

**Q: Why do I not see certain stories on my front page?**
A: There are several possible reasons: the story may have a negative score, you may have hidden it, it may match one of your filtered tags, or its hotness score may have dropped below the threshold.

**Q: What is the difference between "Active" and "Hottest"?**
A: "Hottest" ranks stories by overall engagement (votes, comments, age). "Active" simply shows stories with the most recent comments, regardless of their overall score.

**Q: What is the "Recent" feed?**
A: Recent shows stories from the past 10 days that did not make it to the front page -- stories with lower scores that you might have missed. It is a good place to find hidden gems.

**Q: Can I subscribe to feeds via RSS?**
A: Yes. Most feeds have an RSS version available. Add `.rss` to the URL or look for the RSS link. You can also use a private RSS token to get personalized feeds that respect your tag filters.

**Q: What does the "Last Read" marker mean on the Newest page?**
A: It shows where you left off the last time you visited the Newest page, so you can easily scroll to where new stories begin.

## Tips & Best Practices

- Check the "Recent" feed regularly to find quality stories that may not have gotten enough votes for the front page
- Use tag filters to keep your feeds focused on topics you care about
- Subscribe to RSS feeds for your favorite tags or the front page to stay updated
- Use the Top page with different time intervals to discover the best content over any period
- The Newest page is the best place to find and upvote fresh submissions

---

## Limitations & Important Notes

- You must be logged in to view your Saved, Hidden, and Upvoted feeds
- Feeds show 25 stories per page
- Anonymous visitors see cached versions of feeds (updated every 45 seconds)
- Tag filters for anonymous visitors are stored in a browser cookie and may be lost if cookies are cleared
- The front page shows only stories with positive scores
